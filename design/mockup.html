The problem

A stable with 60+ boxes, its staff and many horse owners. Every request ("extra hay for box 12", "turn him out at 10 tomorrow") went through phone calls, messages and memory. Billing for extra services was done by hand at the end of the month.

My role

Everything: requirements gathering with the owner, UX, backend, frontend, deployment and support. I met the client regularly, turned feedback into small iterations and shipped frequent production releases.

What I built

One app for five roles

Owner, staff, trainer, horse owner and lessee. Each role sees only what it needs, with role-based access control enforced on the API.

Service orders

Horse owners order one-off or subscription services for today or tomorrow. Staff accept, complete or cancel them on a live task board.

Feeding and turnout

Owners pick from a catalogue of ready-made instructions. Staff see today's plan per box and can change times for today only.

Billing

Boarding, camera and lease fees, services, net/gross VAT per box, separate cash and transfer balances. Prices are frozen on each order.

Scheduled jobs

Daily tasks from subscriptions, monthly charges and payment reminders.

Web Push

Notifications with an icon badge counter for staff and owners, working even when the app is closed.

Designed for real users

Some users are over 70. The UI follows strict rules, and feed rooms and paddock exits have wall-mounted screens that show today's plan without anyone touching them.

18 pxminimum text size
48 pxminimum touch target
Aastatus always shown as text, never colour alone

Engineering

  • REST API in NestJS + Prisma/PostgreSQL with email verification, password reset and secure photo uploads.
  • 130+ unit and 100+ end-to-end tests (Jest, Supertest), covering access rules ("a lessee can't see another lessee's fees").
  • CI/CD with GitHub Actions → Docker Compose on a VPS, Caddy for automatic HTTPS, a database backup before every deploy and data-safe migrations.
  • A decision log: every significant change is written down with the reason behind it and the options that were rejected.

Key decision

React Native (Expo)→Installable PWA

A PWA instead of a native app

I started with a React Native (Expo) app. After watching how the owner actually used her phone, we switched to a single installable web app. It's one codebase for every device, needs no app store, and updates instantly. That was the right call, and I'd make it again.
